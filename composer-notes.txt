Aarav & Meera — digital wedding invitation (template)

index.html is a complete, self-contained page. Open it through a local server,
or upload this folder as-is to any static host (Netlify, Vercel, GitHub Pages,
a plain web server). Keep index.html and the assets/ folder together.

Opening index.html by double-clicking shows the paper and the cover, but the
cut-outs will not load — relative asset paths need a server.


THE FLOW
  1. Cover     — the sealed envelope. Exactly one viewport, never scrolls.
  2. Suite     — click the envelope and the flat-lay assembles underneath it.
                 No navigation; the cover cross-fades away in place.
  3. Countdown — scroll on. The olive-grove painting run full-bleed under a
                 dark scrim, a live clock in cream, the names and the date,
                 and a link back to the sealed envelope.

  THE COUNTDOWN HAS THREE STATES, not one:
    counting  days / hours / minutes / seconds until the ceremony
    today     the clock gives way to "Today's the day"
    married   the date has passed — "Married"
  A template outlives its demo date, so a guest must never meet a row of
  zeros. The interval also stops while the tab is hidden.

  The countdown is a BAND (72px / 20px / 78px, no min-height), matching the
  sample invite, and uses that sample's own faces -- Italianno, Cinzel and
  EB Garamond -- held in --cd-* tokens so they reach nothing else.

  assets/countdown-scene.webp (1600px, ~300 KB) is that painting. It is the
  one image on the page that is a photograph rather than a cut-out, so it
  carries the weight it needs to stay soft rather than blocky at full bleed.

  The flat-lay is SIZED BY WIDTH, not fitted to the window, and it is
  FULL-BLEED on a phone and a tablet (--gutter:0) so nothing is lost to side
  margins. .frame carries NO min-height: forcing a full screen there was what
  left a phone showing a small collage floating in a band of empty paper. The
  countdown now follows straight on, and the reader scrolls.

  Capping the stage by window height instead (an earlier attempt) shrank every
  piece on a laptop to about a third of the window -- do not bring that back.

  Piece POSITIONS are never touched by any of this. They are in canvas units,
  so changing the stage width scales the whole collage as one object and the
  layout is identical at every size.


THE SECTIONS ARE PAGES
  Save the Date, Our Story, Wedding Details and RSVP are full pages, not
  dialogs. Each has its own URL:

    #save-the-date   #our-story   #details   #rsvp

  Routing is off location.hash, so the browser's back button, a bookmark and a
  shared link all work with no history bookkeeping. A link straight to a
  section skips the envelope rather than stranding the reader on a cover they
  did not ask for.

  The state attribute sits on <html>, not on .page, because the sections are
  SIBLINGS of .page. While one is open, .page is hidden whole -- hiding only
  its contents still left a blank screen above the section, since .page
  carries min-height:100dvh.

  Sections snap with `y proximity`, never `mandatory`: on a short window the
  suite is taller than the viewport and mandatory would fight the reader.


THE FLAT-LAY IS THE NAVIGATION
  Six pieces are doors. Each carries a .piece__hit button with
  data-opens="<panel id>"; the panels are <dialog> elements below the suite.

    Save-the-Date tag   ->  Save the Date
    Date banner         ->  Save the Date
    RSVP tag            ->  RSVP
    Details card        ->  The Details
    Monogram card       ->  Our Story
    Invitation card     ->  Our Story

  To move a door, change data-opens. To add one, put a .piece__hit button in
  the piece and add .piece--link to the piece's class.


>>> THE RSVP FORM IS NOT CONNECTED <<<
  #rsvp-form has an empty data-endpoint. Until a real endpoint is set, the form
  REFUSES to submit and says so on screen -- deliberately, because a wedding
  site that silently swallows replies is worse than one with no form at all.

  Put any endpoint that accepts a POST of form data in data-endpoint
  (Formspree, Getform, Basin, a Google Form action). Send a test reply and
  confirm it arrives BEFORE giving the link to guests.

  The "Add to calendar" button on Save the Date is a self-contained .ics data
  URI -- no service, nothing to configure -- but it carries the date and venue,
  so update it when those change.


THE WEDDING DATE
  ONE place: data-date on <section class="countdown">, an ISO local datetime
  (currently 2027-11-27T19:00:00, a Saturday). The clock, the printed "27 . 11 . 27" and
  the screen-reader line are all derived from it. It counts down and stops at
  zero -- it never shows a negative number once the day has passed.


SWAPPING IN A COUPLE'S DETAILS
  All of it is live text in the markup -- nothing is baked into an image.

  Cover                .cover__names, .cover__open / .cover__magic
  Countdown            .countdown__names
  Wedding date         data-date on #countdown  (see above)
  Invitation card      .type--invite   names, date, venue
  Details card         .type--details   the DATE / TIME / VENUE rows
  Vow tags             .type--vows      "His Vows" / "Her Vows"
  Monogram             .type--monogram  the two initials
  Date banner          .type--ribbon
  Quote strip          .type--quote
  Name tags            .type--name
  Browser tab + link previews  <title> and <meta name="description">

  The date appears in FIVE places and they are not wired together: the
  countdown's data-date, the invitation card, the details card, the banner and
  the quote-adjacent copy. Change them together.


THE PANELS FOLLOW THE DESIGN FILE
  Three separate token sets, so a change to one cannot reach the others:

    landing page + countdown   --ink-*, --gold-*, --cream-*, --ds-*
    the four panels            --d-*

  The panels follow the Claude Design file: a gold spaced-caps eyebrow, an
  Italiana heading, a line-diamond-line divider, then Cormorant body copy set
  generously -- plus taped polaroids in Our Story and a sprig tile grid in
  Wedding Details. The flat-lay and the countdown reference no --d-* token at
  all, which is what keeps them out of it.

  Italiana (assets/fonts/italiana-400.woff2, 10 KB) is self-hosted like the
  others; the design loaded it from Google Fonts, but nothing here should
  depend on an external request.


HOW TEXT SITS ON A CARD
  A .type block is a box measured in PERCENTAGES OF ITS OWN PIECE
  (--tl left, --tt top, --tw width, --th height, --trot rotation), and its
  font-size is one hundredth of that piece's rendered width. Everything inside
  is therefore in `em`, so a block set once is correct at every screen size and
  the words can never drift off their card.

  To reposition text, change --tl/--tt/--tw/--th on the block -- never the
  font sizes. To check a change, confirm the text still fits at 360px wide and
  at 1920px: those are the extremes.


CANVAS
  Each breakpoint is its own layout, in canvas units, with --u turning one unit
  into real pixels. --ch is the CONTENT height, not the device's.

    phone   (<768px)   390 x 515    stage capped 480px
    tablet  (default)  768 x 1024   stage capped 900px
    desktop (>=1024px) 768 x 1024   stage capped 1000px

  The stage is capped by WINDOW HEIGHT as well as width, so the whole flat-lay
  is on screen at once at every size rather than asking anyone to scroll.


BEFORE A CUSTOMER PUBLISHES
  og:image is a RELATIVE path. WhatsApp, Slack and Twitter resolve that fine;
  Facebook's scraper wants an absolute URL. Once the site has a domain, set
  og:image to the full https://... address and add an og:url to match.

  assets/og-cover.webp (1200x630) is the link preview -- the first thing a
  guest sees when the couple sends the link. It is NOT loaded by the page, so
  it costs nothing at runtime. Regenerate it whenever the names or date change.

  The favicon is an inline SVG data URI in <link rel="icon">, so it is one less
  request and recolours by editing two hex values.


ASSETS
  assets/*.webp     resized to at most 2x their largest rendered size
  _source-assets/   the untouched original PNGs — re-run the export from these
                    if a piece is ever repositioned larger

  Total page weight is ~1.7 MB across 24 requests. It was 40 MB before the
  originals were resized and converted; keep new pieces in .webp and sized to
  what they actually render, or that comes straight back.

composition.ts is the tablet layout as TypeScript constants, in the format the
Next.js invitation site uses in src/data/composition.ts. Its comments still
name the original source files, not the shortened asset names.
